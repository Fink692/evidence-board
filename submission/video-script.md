# Evidence Board — narrated walkthrough draft

**Target runtime: 2 minutes 40 seconds.** Keep the final edit between 2:35 and 2:45. Evidence Board is the working title, pending human confirmation.

This is a recording plan, not evidence that a video has been created or uploaded. Any local silent capture or synthetic-narration file is an optional draft asset until its playback, timing, captions, and factual accuracy have been verified. A silent capture does not complete the narrated-video requirement.

## Before recording

Use a fresh fictional library case and a readable desktop viewport. Keep the question, source text, review controls, and brief legible. Record real interactions; do not insert simulated agent typing, artificial thinking delays, or a fake “connected” badge.

For a native-registration shot, use a browser in which the setup panel actually confirms registration. The recorded local verification used Chromium 151.0.7922.34 with `--enable-blink-features=WebMCP,WebMCPTesting`. A default browser launch may show “Agent setup”; that is valid fallback behaviour. If native registration cannot be shown in the recording, use the tool catalogue and the separately verified test result instead, with an explicit caption. Do not present a static catalogue or the scripted rehearsal as a live model connection.

The narration below is approximately 350 words, or 130 words per minute at the target runtime. Rehearse once, then adjust pauses and cuts to fit the timeline without concealing the review or approval step.

## 00:00–00:16 — The decision and the weak assumption

**Picture:** Start on the evidence map. Hold the research question long enough to read. Select “Demand justifies overnight opening.”

**Narration:**

“Evidence Board gives a research decision room to be questioned. This fictional university is considering longer library hours during exams. The current argument looks plausible, but do its sources actually justify keeping the building open all night?”

## 00:16–00:36 — Read the source, including its limits

**Picture:** Select “72% want later library hours” and open its source. Then show “Only 8% of entries occur after midnight” and its excerpt. Keep the fictional-source label visible.

**Narration:**

“Seventy-two percent of voluntary survey respondents wanted later hours. The question did not specify how late. Meanwhile, only eight percent of trial entries occurred after midnight. That is a share of entries, not occupancy or unmet demand. The original wording stays visible beside the claim.”

## 00:36–00:55 — Show the real integration surface

**Picture:** Open “Agent setup” and, only if actually available, show “10 native tools registered.” Then show “Activity & tools” → “Tool catalogue.” Open one read tool and `propose_change_set`.

**Caption:** “Native WebMCP registration/invocation tested separately · No external model used in this walkthrough.”

**Narration:**

“Ten structured WebMCP tools expose this shared board to a compatible browser agent. Native registration and invocation have been tested in Chromium. The setup panel reports actual availability. Tools can inspect evidence and prepare proposals, but none can approve a change.”

## 00:55–01:13 — Be explicit about the rehearsal

**Picture:** Return to “Evidence board” and click “Run demo rehearsal.” Show the review tray as it appears. Do not add a fake delay.

**Caption:** “Scripted rehearsal · Same real handlers · No AI inference.”

**Narration:**

“For this walkthrough, I’m using Run demo rehearsal: a fixed six-call script through those same handlers, with no model or API key. It proposes three changes and leaves every one for human review. Nothing has entered the accepted record.”

## 01:13–01:38 — Edit and select, rather than accepting everything

**Picture:** Show all three cards. On “Connect the overlooked usage evidence,” click “Edit wording.” Use the text below and click “Save wording.” Uncheck “Separate exam weeks from the baseline.” Keep the link and conflict selected; show “2 of 3 selected.”

**Suggested human edit:** “Entry counts question the overnight assumption, but do not measure occupied seats or unmet need.”

**Narration:**

“The first proposal connects overlooked counterevidence. The second makes the survey–usage tension explicit. The third adds a question about exam weeks versus ordinary weeks. Each has its own rationale and proposed result. I’ll keep the link and conflict, make the explanation more careful, and leave the new question out.”

## 01:38–01:55 — Make the approval boundary visible

**Picture:** Click “Apply selected (2).” Return to the map and show the new challenge link and accepted conflict. Keep the content revision visible where possible.

**Narration:**

“Only when I click Apply selected does the accepted board change. The unselected question is rejected. Invalid references or an outdated revision are checked before a proposal or approval is committed, so a failed operation cannot leave half a change behind.”

## 01:55–02:15 — Export the accepted record

**Picture:** Click “Create brief.” Show the cited evidence and accepted conflict. Point out “Only what you accepted.” Use “Export .md” if the capture includes a real local download; otherwise hover or show the control without claiming a download.

**Narration:**

“The decision brief is a deterministic, cited snapshot of the accepted board. The accepted link and conflict are included; the rejected research question is absent. I can copy it or export Markdown. This is the evidence record we reviewed, not a fresh AI answer.”

## 02:15–02:31 — Trace the work and reverse the decision

**Picture:** Briefly show “Activity & tools,” including a “Demo rehearsal” badge and one expanded input/result. Return to the board, click “Undo,” then switch to “List.”

**Narration:**

“Activity identifies the rehearsal and records tool inputs and results. Undo restores the previous accepted content. A structured list and JSON backup keep the same research usable even when native browser-agent support is unavailable.”

## 02:31–02:40 — Close on the product

**Picture:** Hold a clean board or list frame. Show the working name and a simple end card. Add a demo URL only after that exact public URL has been deployed and checked.

**Narration:**

“Evidence Board makes a second perspective inspectable. Follow the sources, review the changes, and keep your judgement.”
